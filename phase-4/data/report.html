Phase 4 — Problem definition

The Stalled Shortlister cannot finish a judgement inside the wishlist — neither which of these, nor is it worth the price — so intent parks on a sale that may never arrive.

North star: Wishlist-to-Purchase within 30 days (W2P 30d) · Constraint: No monetary incentive as the core lever

The six required fields

Target user segment

The Stalled Shortlister — a shopper who saves several items they genuinely like, returns to the list, and leaves without deciding because nothing in the list helps them finish a judgement. 5/9 convert sometimes or less, 6/9 compare inside the app, and confidence that a saved item is right for them averages 2.89 (range 0–6). Fit-led S2 gives way to compare-led S4, with fit secondary.

Evidence
  • survey:Q5 — 5/9 buy from saves sometimes or less
  • survey:Q11 — 6/9 compare in-app, 8/9 read reviews or photos
  • survey:Q9 — confidence mean 2.89, minimum 0
  • survey:Q3 ∩ Q5 — 2/9 match the strict staller definition (r03, r07)
Caveats
  • In-segment n = 2 against a target of 5. Segment-specific claims rest on 2 people and are labelled throughout. This is the weakest part of the lock.
  • S3 sale-watchers stay a control persona, not the audience.

Product outcome

Shortlist-to-decision rate — the share of saved items that reach an explicit decision, bought or deliberately dropped, within 30 days of being saved. That is the decide node, not resolve: 8/9 of respondents show an unfinished decide job against 7/9 for resolve. Uncertainty resolution rate is the secondary outcome. A deliberate removal counts as success, so the metric cannot be moved by pressure tactics.

Evidence
  • survey:Q11 in-app comparison, Q7 save-to-compare, Q13 comparison or price-verdict help — decide: 8/9
  • survey:Q10 fit/size/appearance or quality/material doubt, Q13 quality or review help — resolve: 7/9
  • phase2:metric-tree.json — Revisit × uncertainty resolution × decision completion decompose W2P 30d
Caveats
  • Thin margin: decide leads resolve by 1 respondent. The case for decide rests on the unlock-versus-help evidence in the root cause, not on this share alone, and the two nodes are close enough that the secondary outcome must ship alongside the primary rather than after it.
  • Guardrail: return rate must not rise, and removal-heavy cohorts must not convert worse than untouched ones.

Root cause

A wishlist captures interest but offers no way to finish a judgement — neither "which of these is right for me" nor "is this worth what it costs". With no judgement aid available, the only decision tool the app offers is a falling price, so intent parks on a sale that may never arrive inside 30 days. Price is the symptom of a missing capability, not a demand for money.

Evidence
  • survey:Q8 vs Q11 — 5/9 name price as the main blocker, but only 2/9 actually wait for a sale and 1/9 do nothing else
  • survey:Q12 → Q13 — all 4 discount-seekers (r01, r03, r05, r07) ask for information when offered help; 3 ask specifically to understand whether the price is good
  • survey:Q11 — 4/4 of the discount-seekers research or compare before buying anyway — the non-tautological half of the finding
  • survey:Q13 — top request for help is "Understanding whether the price is good" (4/9)
Caveats
  • Challenged in segment: 2/2 in-segment respondents chose a discount as the unlock.
  • Mitigated by the same respondents: 2/2 of them asked for a price verdict on Q13.
  • Unresolved by instrument design: price was never held constant. Until brief questions 3 and 4 are asked as written, a real price constraint cannot be separated from the easiest available answer.
  • The constraint conflict is navigable but not resolved.

Existing workarounds

Shoppers already finish the judgement outside the wishlist: read customer reviews (8/9), look at customer photos (7/9), compare with other products on the same app (6/9), check size/fit reviews (5/9). 4 of the reported behaviours leave the app entirely, and easy returns are used as a substitute for deciding.

Evidence

  • discovery:review-trust-gap — Shoppers leave for YouTube and Instagram try-ons before buying
Caveats
  • Every workaround is a judgement made outside the product, which is why they read as evidence of the missing capability rather than as satisfied need.

User value

Closure. A defensible choice — knowing why this one and not the other two — and a defensible price, which 5/9 ask for and nothing currently answers. Fewer open loops: no second app, no group chat, no video detour before ordering. Confidence is the mechanism; the felt benefit is finishing.

Evidence
  • survey:Q13 + Q12 — 5/9 ask for a value verdict on either question
  • survey:Q9 — confidence mean 2.89 — the doubt is real before the purchase, not after
Caveats
  • Framing matters: an AI that announces which items are worth buying scores only 2.89 (min 0), so the value must be delivered as evidence, not as a verdict from the app.

Business value

W2P 30d rises through decision completion rather than margin: every point of shortlist-to-decision rate is a purchase that would otherwise have decayed, bought at full price. Margin is protected because the intervention is information, and nothing trains shoppers to wait for the next sale. Return-rate exposure falls where fit doubt is resolved before ordering, and demand shifts out of sale windows if waiting is a default rather than a preference.

Evidence
  • survey:Q11 — 2/9 wait for a sale today — the volume that could move into non-sale weeks
  • discovery:return-fear — try-and-buy used as the decision, frequency 0.383
  • phase2:nomination.json — price levers were already excluded upstream: Price-drop / sale alerts (monetary — excluded by assignment constraint)
Caveats
  • All four claims are conditional on the root cause being right, and the root cause states how it can be shown wrong.

Segment contract (interface only — apps/mvp/lib/segment.ts (Phase 5a))

// Generated by phase-4. Contract only — Phase 5a implements this against real user data.
export function matchesStalledShortlister(user: User): boolean {
  if (user.optedOut) return false;

  const recent = user.wishlistItems.filter((i) => i.addedWithinDays(30));
  if (recent.length < 2) return false;

  const purchased = recent.filter((i) => i.purchasedWithinDays(30));
  if (purchased.length > 1) return false;

  const byCategory = groupByCategory(recent);
  return Object.values(byCategory).some((count) => count >= 2);
}
  • Window stays 30 days: W2P 30d is the north-star metric, so eligibility is measured on the same clock.
  • Save floor lowered from 3 to 2: the modal bucket is "1–5" (6/9), and a three-item floor would exclude most of the population.
  • Comparison signal set to 2 items in one category: two comparable options are enough to be stuck between them, and 6/9 compare inside the app.
  • At most one purchase in the window: the segment is defined by the stall, and 5/9 convert sometimes or less.
  • Opt-out is checked first, before any wishlist data is read.
